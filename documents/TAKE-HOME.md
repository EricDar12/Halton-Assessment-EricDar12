# Take-Home Assignment

## Objective

The goal of this take-home is to understand how you:
- Approach an unfamiliar dataset
- Explore and reason through data quality and structure
- Communicate findings clearly
- Present insights in a way that makes sense for the work you chose to do

This is not a test of specific tools or advanced engineering. We care more about your thinking, clarity, and how you explain what you did.

---

## Problem Statement

The Government of Canada publishes contract information for contracts over $10,000.

Your task is to use this dataset to answer:

**“What are the most important and actionable insights we can learn from this contract data?”**

---

## What You Need To Do

### 1) Load and explore the dataset
- Use the provided setup and notebook to connect to the dataset.
- Explore the available fields, missing values, and any inconsistencies you notice.

### 2) Analyze the data
- Create notebooks to query, transform, and explore the data.
- Break the data up however you think is most useful (derived tables, subsets, summaries, etc.).
- Focus on insights you believe are meaningful.

### 3) Present your findings
Create a concise deliverable that summarizes your findings and approach.

Presentation formats can include (but are not limited to):
- Slide deck (PowerPoint / Google Slides / PDF)
- Written report (Markdown / PDF)
- Notebook “report” section with clear narrative + visuals
- A lightweight dashboard or interactive view using free tools
- Any other creative format that helps communicate what you found

We encourage creative approaches (not just a standard slide deck), as long as the result is clear and easy to review.

---

## What To Submit

### Submission (GitHub repository only)
Upload this project to a GitHub repository and share the link.

Your submission must include **everything required for review** in the repository, including:
- Notebooks (analysis, exploration, queries)
- Source code and any scripts you create
- Any generated outputs you include (small summary tables, charts, etc.)
- Your presentation/report deliverable file(s) (slides, PDF, markdown report, etc.)

Ensure your notebooks and deliverables are present in the repo and working as expected before submitting.

---

## What We’re Looking For

You do **not** need to answer these exact questions, but strong submissions often include elements like:

- Clear exploration of what the dataset contains and how you validated your understanding
- A small number of insights that are well-supported by queries/results
- Identification of interesting patterns or anomalies and why they might matter
- Awareness of data quality issues (nulls, inconsistent formats, outliers) and how you handled them
- Clear explanations of assumptions, tradeoffs, and limitations
- Practical next steps: what you’d investigate next, or what questions you’d ask to go deeper

---

## Notes / Guidelines

- Leverage free/open tools.
- Do not commit the raw dataset file itself into version control (keep it local in the `data/` folder).
- Be clear about assumptions, limitations, and any data issues you encountered.
- Keep your final deliverable concise and easy to understand.
